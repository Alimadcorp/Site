history of me, i guess

Muhammad Ali is a 17 year old developer and student from Pakistan, who likes to make websites, apps, games, and is currently most interested in writing secure programs. Muhammad Ali is also known as Alimad, Alimad Co, or Alimad Corporations, which is his self-made company (which isn't registered yet, lol).

He was born in a Muslim household on the 12th of October 2008, in Sharaqpur, Pakistan. When he was a kid, he used to have his uncle's computer, which he completely used to take over on, and not let his brother use it. He liked computers from the start. In fifth class, he got intrigued by his cousin Arman to join YouTube. Around the same time, he used to make drawings and crafts at home, so he started a YouTube channel named "Art and Craft Ideas". It went well for 2 years—not so popular, like around 400 subs—but he eventually deleted the channel (doesn't particularly remember why, but he was a kid so lol). The only video that was recovered and republished to his new channel was this unique craft video.

Then, in 2018, his friend used the new Microsoft Video Editor that came preinstalled with Windows (prior to ClipChamp). His friend drew a bunch of Minecraft pics with MS Paint and put them together as an animation on the editor, and they published it on his channel. Then they made episode 2 of it, and then Muhammad Ali made episode 3 of it—but this time, he took about 40 days working on the episode inside Techsmith Camtasia, finally releasing it on his new channel (now youtube.com/@alimadco) at Camtasia Minecraft Animation Episode 3.

A few weeks passed by, and while watching YouTube, he saw a video about a new device where the creator showed a 3D model of it to explain how it works. Even just for a second, he caught a glimpse of the software used to show the model: "Autodesk Maya". 3D editing software actually exists? OMG ITS PAID?? A few days later, he was watching a Black Plasma Studios creation and found out that they use a different sort of editor called Blender. It was a dream come true! He opened it up and learned from a bunch of tutorials. Then he went to his cousin, who had an actual computer, and they made their first Blender animation together. They didn't even know how to export back then, so they screen-recorded the playback and published it to YouTube at their first Blender animation video.

From thereon, there was a lot of learning Blender and making random animations, including a significant Blender Architectural Model showcase. They also made random YouTube videos on Camtasia, creating a series called The Crash Technology series, as well as an Introduction to Computer Graphics playlist, and many more! This workflow continued for about 2 years.

Then, in 2022, he found a button. A button at the top right of his Blender editor. (He used to run Blender 2.79b on his laptop because it didn't support the latest OpenGL 3.1 version. If it hadn't been for his laptop's oldness, he might never have gotten into this, but anyhow!) It was called "Game Engine". He clicked it, switching directly to the Blender Game Engine (BGE). Out of curiosity, he watched a tutorial, learned how to make something, and built his first prototype: a basic local multiplayer goal-making game called Makeways, originally released on 7/6/2022 at 1:50 pm PKT. Initially, he shared it with friends (who wouldn't actually play it) via MEGA, before switching over to itch.io, where it launched on Feb 23, 2023, at 03:48 UTC at alimad.itch.io/makeways. He continued to make games in BGE for 2 more years, his latest one being Swiveling Escape available at alimad.itch.io/swiveling-escape. In total, he published 8 Blender games before switching to Godot because he needed mobile support—BECAUSE LITERALLY NO ONE PLAYED HIS GAMES.

Now starts the Godot era. His first-ever game was a little too simple, released on Jul 11, 2023, called Polk (alimad.itch.io/polk), followed quite immediately by a family favorite on Jul 14 called Buggle (alimad.itch.io/buggle), which is a bubble-popping game that gets faster over time. Then, he ran his first game jam (yeah, didn't join his first game jam, he ran a game jam before joining one!) called the Alimad Banana Game Jam! He made a game for his own jam called The Minions Resurrection over at alimad.itch.io/theminions. Eventually, he also got into learning Java for Processing, where he made a triangle area calculating app, followed by a Pendulum Calculation app on itch.io. (At this time he was in 9th class; by the end of it, this work got a bit hard to maintain as his parents got extreme at him for wasting his time doing stuff online).

He also made a really fun and addictive local multiplayer game in Processing called Madarea (alimad.itch.io/madarea). His last Godot game was published on Sep 25, 2023, named Madrun, which was a board game based on a physical board game he had crafted one year prior, found at alimad.itch.io/madrun. Anyhow, eventually a year later, he realized that Godot wasn't enough either (he hadn't exported any of these games to Android as he hadn't been able to, so instead, the web builds were simply played on phones).

At 10 PM on March 18th, 2024, right after his last exam of Urdu in 10th class, he put Unity on download on his new laptop. It was Ramadan. At 3 AM the next morning, it finished downloading, finally. He opened it up; it was quite a bit difficult to learn, but eventually, he made his sample game called Shootmad (alimad.itch.io/shootmad). Then, a few days later, he decided to join a game jam. Submitting to the Interstellar Game Jam 6, he built the Zolitude itch.io game, which received quite a positive live review on this developer stream. It was also Eid-ul-Azha that day, making it his first time actually joining an online community of devs! He also uploaded a devlog video on it at his Zolitude YouTube video.

Later on, he joined the Fox Hollow Jam 2 and submitted a terrible game called Banana (alimad.itch.io/banana), but it led to a lot of growth eventually due to their amazing community. He made Zomboozle (alimad.itch.io/zomboozle) for the next jam ("Zombeez Jam") and didn't win their $500 prize. Then he created Ikeamad (alimad.itch.io/ikeamad) for Lame Jam 42—didn't make it to the top, but learned a lot. Eventually, he became friends with DataPotato, a creator from the Interstellar Game Jam, and made a YouTube collab with them at this collab video link.

A year later, he joined the Fox Hollow Jam 3, made Chromadicc (alimad.itch.io/chromadicc), and won $75, haha! You can watch their live stream feedback on his game via this stream link. He purchased Minecraft with the prize money instead of paypalling it all, because PayPal doesn't work in Pakistan. Later, the host of the jam had to leave game jam hosting because he got employed, and he sure was really sad about it; a lot went down in this silent little video: watch video here. He also joined the GMTK Jam 2024 (alimad.itch.io/gmtkmad-2024), GMTK Jam 2025 (alimad.itch.io/gmtk25), and fully plans to join the 2026 jam too.

While all this was ongoing, in December of 2024 during his first year, they ran into a problem at college (Government College University, Lahore). They conduct a test every year called the sendup, managing about 3,000 students in the 1st year across evening and morning shifts. On the day of the exam, the seating plan was provided as a long, 40-page PDF in a range format ("roll numbers in this range sit here"). Students had to manually scroll through to find their name and seat for each of the 7 or 8 tests, and text searching failed completely because a standard finder won't catch "3686" inside a written range like "3600-3712". So, Muhammad Ali came up with an idea to fix this. He built an HTML web app that takes the CSV contents extracted from the PDF and dynamically calculates the exact seating plan of a student given their roll number. This app became insanely famous, with about 70% of the college students coming to find their seating plans. It even had a comments section filled with positive feedback before getting deleted. The PDF parsing was handled via Python (his first time using it), the frontend was plain HTML, and the backend was almost nonexistent since he used a direct Firebase config directly inside the static HTML file to hit the database :sob:. Anyhoo, that went real well, prompting him to jump into web dev. In the start, he built a couple of frontend sites using v0 :sob:, but by the next year, he transitioned into learning real web dev. He is now much better at it, even if he very rarely runs into small issues due to how unstructured his web dev journey started.

By February 06, 2025, he learned of Hack Club from a college friend and joined the community immediately. Hack Club is a global community of tech teens who code together, and the non-profit organization rewards them with hardware, domains, and tools for building personal projects. He jumped into working right after his 1st-year exams. His first project was a Unity game called "GleamBox", which turned out to be a massive success; however, because he ground away at it every day for 2 weeks, he completely burned out and left the project behind. One of the playtesters even came forward asking to join the repo to help with development because they loved the concept so much! Eventually, he shifted over to web development projects (not to mention leaving behind 20 other planned projects due to exam time constraints). He built his first web project and earned a domain grant, purchasing his primary domain alimad.co from Hack Club. Over the next 2 years, he made more and more platforms, currently managing about 30 subdomains or more, mwahahhahaaa! His YouTube channel is now highly active, while his itch.io page remains mostly inactive.

During his time participating in the Winter Melon Game Jam, he made a great friend named Finn Awen Nijpels, with whom he developed the game MagnetMad together. They remain close friends and still play Minecraft together regularly.

Looking ahead, Muhammad Ali has completed his FAST university entry test and secured an excellent aggregate score. Inshallah, he will be pursuing his BSCS degree from 2026 to 2030, diving deep into the computer science department, taking on plenty of freelancing opportunities along the way, and continuing his journey with Hack Club since he remains under 18 until next year in October 2027. Love and peace ✌️
